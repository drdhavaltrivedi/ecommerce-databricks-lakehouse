# Demo video — shot list and captions

Two jobs, one recording:

1. **Attract loop** — plays on the booth screen when nobody is standing there,
   and has to stop someone mid-walk.
2. **Fallback** — when the wifi dies or the warehouse is cold, you switch to
   this and keep talking over it without missing a beat.

## The constraint that shapes everything

**A trade show hall is loud, and nobody will hear your audio.** Seamless is a
big, noisy room. So:

- **Record silent.** No voiceover. Do not rely on sound for anything.
- **Every point must be an on-screen caption**, large enough to read from
  2–3 metres.
- **It must loop cleanly** — no fade to black, no end card that sits there dead.
- Target **around 75 seconds**. Much longer and the loop feels slow when
  someone catches it mid-way; the cut below lands at 74s.

Record at **1920×1080**, browser zoom **150%** (booth screens are viewed from
much further than a desk), cursor visible, everything else on screen hidden —
no bookmarks bar, no notifications, no other tabs.

## Shot list

What is **on screen**, shot by shot. The exact caption wording for each is in
[Version A](#version-a--silent-booth-loop-captions-word-for-word) below — the
timings in both tables match, so cut against either.

Total **74s**.

| # | Time | On screen |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 0:00–0:05 | Booth dashboard, top KPI row, still |
| 2 | 0:05–0:12 | Slow pan across the four counters |
| 3 | 0:12–0:24 | Hold on the $99.5M counter |
| 4 | 0:24–0:30 | Scroll down to the price-band chart, settle |
| 5 | 0:30–0:42 | Hold on chart; cursor traces the flat teal bars left→right |
| 6 | 0:42–0:54 | Same chart; cursor rests on the short pink bars at the bottom |
| 7 | 0:54–1:02 | Cut to Genie. Type the question live, at readable speed |
| 8 | 1:02–1:08 | Genie's answer appears. Hold |
| 9 | 1:08–1:14 | Hold on the caveat sentence inside that answer |

Then cut straight back to shot 1. No end card, no logo sting — it loops.

The cursor work in shots 5 and 6 is doing real work: tracing the flat bars makes
"it doesn't fall" a thing the viewer *sees* rather than reads, and moving to the
short bars sets up the shipping-cost point without needing a caption for it.

### The Genie question to type in shot 7

Type this one, character by character, at readable speed:

> **Should we discount more to boost conversion?**

**This is the most important shot in the video.** The answer comes back with the
numbers *and* the sentence about the result being confounded and needing a
holdout test. Shot 9 exists purely to hold on that sentence.

Anyone in that hall who has been sold an AI tool is quietly worried about it
producing confident nonsense. Ten seconds of it visibly refusing to overclaim
does more than any feature list.

# The script, word for word

Two versions. **Version A is the one you record for the booth.** Version B is
for the same footage re-cut with audio, for LinkedIn, the website, or a
follow-up email — places where sound actually works.

---

## Version A — silent booth loop (captions, word for word)

Type these **exactly**. Every line is one caption. Keep them short enough to
read in one glance; if a line does not fit on one screen line at that size,
cut a word, don't shrink the type.

| Shot | In | Out | Caption — set exactly this text |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 0:00 | 0:05 | `109,950,743 real shopping events` |
| 2 | 0:05 | 0:12 | `Two months. One online store.` |
| 3 | 0:12 | 0:20 | `$99.5M left sitting in abandoned carts` |
| 3b | 0:20 | 0:24 | `43% of everything the store earned` |
| 4 | 0:24 | 0:30 | `Everyone assumes that's a pricing problem` |
| 5 | 0:30 | 0:34 | `It isn't.` |
| 5b | 0:34 | 0:42 | `Cart conversion is flat from $50 to $1,000+` |
| 6 | 0:42 | 0:48 | `20× the price. Same conversion.` |
| 6b | 0:48 | 0:54 | `So it's checkout friction — not cost` |
| 7 | 0:54 | 1:02 | *(no caption — the typing is the message)* |
| 8 | 1:02 | 1:08 | `Ask it anything. Plain English.` |
| 9 | 1:08 | 1:14 | `And it tells you when the data can't prove it` |

**Caption style**: white text, semi-transparent dark bar behind it, bottom
third, one line each. `It isn't.` should be noticeably larger than the rest —
it's the turn in the argument and deserves the emphasis.

## Version B — narrated cut (voiceover, word for word)

**135 words.** That is ~62s of speech at a normal 130 wpm, or ~70s read
unhurried at 115 wpm; the marked pauses add roughly 10s on top, landing the cut
at **70–80 seconds**. Read it slower than feels natural — demo voiceovers are
almost always rushed.

`[ ]` = pause. `**bold**` = land on this word.

> A hundred and ten million shopping events. `[beat]` Two months, one online store.
>
> `[pause]`
>
> Ninety-nine and a half million dollars was left sitting in abandoned carts.
> That's **forty-three percent** of everything the store actually earned.
>
> `[pause]`
>
> Now — everybody assumes that's a pricing problem.
>
> `[beat]`
>
> It **isn't**.
>
> `[pause — let the chart land]`
>
> This is cart conversion by price. Fifty dollars. A hundred. Five hundred.
> A thousand and up.
>
> `[beat]`
>
> It's **flat**. Around fifty percent, the whole way across.
>
> `[pause]`
>
> Twenty times the price, same conversion. If people were walking away because
> things cost too much — that line would fall. It doesn't.
>
> `[pause]`
>
> So it isn't cost. It's **friction at checkout**. That's a completely different
> fix, and a much cheaper one.
>
> `[pause]`
>
> And you don't need an analyst to go find that.
>
> `[typing on screen — say nothing for three seconds]`
>
> You just ask.
>
> `[pause]`
>
> It gives you the numbers. And when the data **can't** prove something —
> it says so.

### Notes for reading it

- **"It isn't."** is the whole video. Full stop before it, full stop after.
  Do not rush past it.
- **Say nothing while the question types.** Three seconds of silence with text
  appearing on screen is more convincing than narrating it.
- **Do not end on a sales line.** No "get in touch", no "book a demo". The last
  line is the credibility line and it should be the last thing they hear.
- Numbers spoken as words ("ninety-nine and a half million"), never read out as
  digits. It sounds human and it is easier to follow.

### If you want a 30-second cut

Keep only these, in order — it still works:

> A hundred and ten million shopping events. Three hundred sixty-six million
> dollars left in abandoned carts, in November alone. `[pause]` Everybody
> assumes that's a pricing problem. `[beat]` It **isn't**. Cart conversion is
> flat from fifty dollars to over a thousand. Twenty times the price, same
> conversion. `[pause]` It's friction at checkout, not cost. `[pause]` And you
> can just ask it, in plain English.

## Recording it

```bash
# 1. warm the warehouse first, or the recording captures a spinner
python3 scripts/keep_warm.py
```

Wait for two clean pings, then:

- macOS: **⌘⇧5** → Record Selected Portion → crop to the browser window
- Pre-load the booth dashboard **before** hitting record, so nothing loads on camera
- Pre-run the Genie question once before recording, so shot 7's answer is
  cache-warm and comes back fast
- Do the whole thing in **one take** if you can — cuts between tools look
  stitched, and a continuous take reads as "this is just working"

### Then

- Add captions in any editor (or Canva / CapCut). White text, dark scrim behind
  it, bottom third, large.
- Export **MP4, H.264, 1080p**.
- Save it **locally on the booth laptop.** Not Drive, not Dropbox. The entire
  point is that it survives the wifi dying.
- Also put a copy on a phone, in case the laptop is the thing that fails.

---

## Using it at the stand

**As the attract loop**: muted, looping, fullscreen on the booth screen whenever
nobody is mid-conversation.

**As the fallback**: the moment something hangs for more than a few seconds,
switch to it and keep talking. Do not narrate a loading spinner and do not
apologise for it — most visitors will not register that anything went wrong.

> "Here — this is it running on the full 110 million rows."

Then carry on with the same script from [`BOOTH_DEMO.md`](BOOTH_DEMO.md). The
video covers the same beats in the same order, so your words still line up.

---

## What not to put in it

- **No voiceover.** Nobody will hear it.
- **No architecture diagram.** That is the second conversation, and it is not
  what stops someone walking past.
- **No stock footage of shoppers or warehouses.** It signals marketing, and the
  credibility here comes from the numbers being real.
- **No claim this is client data.** It's public Kaggle data from a real
  retailer — the provenance line is already on the one-pager and should stay
  visible in shot 1 if it happens to be in frame.
